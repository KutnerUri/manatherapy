what we actually know about the weak force?

it's specifically 3 interactions:
the "W-" interaction: neutron --> proton + electron + antineutrino + energy
the "W+" interaction: proton + electron + antineutrino + energy --> neutron (that is a mathematically equivalent oversimplification. technically ,we have this interaction - proton --> neutron + positron + neutrino + absorption of energy energy absorbed)).
the "Z" interaction: particle + energy -> particle + energy (transfer of momentum between particles)

these interactions happen in extremely small scales of time and space.
- The W- interaction, known as "beta decay", is the clearest version of the Weak Force - neutrons naturally decay into protons + electrons, emitting antineutrinos and energy. This is so favorable an interaction that free neutrons typically decay within 15 minutes.
- The W+ interaction happens during atomic fusion - under enough pressure and heat, protons turn into neutrons and, almost simultaneously, fuse to make heavier elements via the strong force. (If you don't know what the strong force is, look for a future article, but it's much simpler)
- The Z interaction only transfers momentum between particles without changing their composition.

The unit - what is changed?

The Weak Force unit is "flavor", or more technically, "weak isospin". (I am not making these up, I swear)

If the electric force has a one-axis charge — proton = +1 and electron = -1; then the weak force has weak isospin. But weak isospin has two axes, giving us: [1,0] (up quark, neutrino), [0,1] (down quark, electron), [-1,0] (anti-up quark, antineutrino), and [0,-1] (anti-down quark, positron). You can only occupy one corner of weak isospin space, like you can only be on one side of electric charge.

In textbook, you will see the notation +1/2 and -1/2 instead of [1,0] and [0,1], which I find unnecessarily confusing, even if they are more useful in electric charge. My guess is that the -1/2 and +1/2 are carried over from spin mathematics where it was used in electric charge calculations.

The W- interaction is the change of a down-quark to an up-quark: [0,1] -> [1,0]. Basically, it's a rotation. There is no equivalent in electricity because rotation requires more than one axis.

The full W- interaction can be described as:

(neutron: [1,0], [0,1], [0,1]) → (proton: [1,0], [1,0], [0,1]) + (electron: [0,1]) + (antineutrino: -[1,0]) + energy.

One quark rotated, releasing energy, and everything else is conservation of weak isospin. Wild, isn't it?

We don't see the weak force mediating interactions between particles much because the scale of interaction is smaller than the size of a proton, preventing it from traveling between particles.

Relation to the electric force:

electric charge = weak isospin + ½ hypercharge

(hypercharge is a separate intrinsic charge each particle carries, independent of weak isospin. we won't go into it here, but it's where the fractional charges of quarks come from)

**The deeper stuff — why the weak force matters beyond decay:**

- The weak force is the only force that **violates mirror symmetry**. It only acts on left-handed chirality particles. This means the universe genuinely behaves differently from its mirror image. Nobody fully understands why. I hypothesize that it's because chirality = helicity, but I have no way to back it up, so I'm not going to explain that claim. 
- This asymmetry between matter and antimatter (known as the "CP violation") is the reason matter exists at all. The Big Bang is hypothesized to have produced nearly equal amounts of both. The weak force's asymmetry produced a tiny surplus of matter — roughly one part per billion — and everything you see is that rounding error.

Chirality is a mathematical property close to, but not the same as, helicity — the alignment of a particle's spin relative to its direction of travel. Unlike helicity, chirality is frame-independent. This asymmetry is experimentally confirmed and unexplained.
